Depreciation

Understanding how values decrease over time using sequences and series

Reducing Balance Depreciation

A geometric sequence approach to asset value decay

Depreciation is the decrease in the value of an asset over time. The reducing balance method (also called declining balance) applies a fixed percentage rate to the current value each period this creates a geometric sequence.

Vn = V0 · (1 − r)n
where V0 = initial value, r = depreciation rate, n = number of periods

The Geometric Sequence

Each year, the value is multiplied by the same factor (1 - r). This forms a geometric sequence with first term V0 and common ratio (1 - r).

Example: A car bought for $20,000 depreciates at 15% per year. The sequence of values is:
$20,000 ; $17,000 ; $14,450 ; $12,282.50 ; (each term is 85% of the previous).

Sum of Depreciation (Series)

The total depreciation over n years is the sum of the geometric series of yearly depreciation amounts. The depreciation in year k is:

Dk = V0 · r · (1 − r)k-1

The sum after n years is a finite geometric series with first term V0 r and ratio (1-r):

Total Depreciation = V0 · [ 1 − (1 − r)n ]

Car Depreciation

Initial value: $25,000, rate: 12% p.a. After 4 years:
V = 25,000 (0.88) = $15,008.64
Total depreciation: $9,991.36

Equipment Depreciation

Initial value: $8,000, rate: 20% p.a. After 3 years:
V = 8,000 (0.80) = $4,096.00
Total depreciation: $3,904.00

Real-World Application

Businesses use reducing-balance depreciation for tax purposes because it matches higher depreciation in early years with higher maintenance costs later. It's also used for vehicles, machinery, electronics anything that loses value more quickly when new.

Key Takeaway

  • Reducing-balance depreciation follows a geometric sequence.
  • The common ratio is (1 - r), where r is the depreciation rate.
  • The total depreciation over n years is a finite geometric series.
  • This model is widely used in accounting, finance, and asset management.
