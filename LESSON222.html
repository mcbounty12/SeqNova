Population Growth

Lesson 02  •  Modeling Real-World Growth with Geometric Sequences

1. Why Population Growth is Geometric

In many real-life situations, populations do not grow by a fixed number of individuals each year. Instead, they grow by a roughly constant percentage of the current population. This type of growth produces a geometric sequence.

When a population increases (or decreases) by a fixed percentage each time period, the population sizes form a geometric sequence, and the total growth can be modeled using geometric series and exponential formulas.

Examples of populations that often follow geometric growth include:

  • Bacteria and microorganisms
  • Human populations in certain periods
  • Animal populations under ideal conditions
  • Spread of information or viruses

2. The Geometric Growth Model

Let:

  • P0 = initial population
  • r = growth rate per time period (as a decimal)
  • n = number of time periods
  • Pn = population after n periods

The population after n periods is given by the geometric sequence formula:

Pn = P0 × (1 + r)n

Here, the common ratio of the geometric sequence is (1 + r).

Note: If the population is decreasing (for example, due to emigration or decline), then r is negative and 0 < (1 + r) < 1.

3. Finding Future Population

Example 1 – Basic Population Projection

A town has a population of 12 000 people. The population is growing at a rate of 4% per year. What will the population be after 6 years?

Given:
P0 = 12 000
r = 4% = 0.04
n = 6
Solution:
P6 = 12 000 × (1 + 0.04)6
P6 = 12 000 × (1.04)6
P6 = 12 000 × 1.265319
P6 ≈ 15 184

After 6 years, the population will be approximately 15 184 people.

Example 2 – Bacterial Growth

A colony of bacteria starts with 500 bacteria and doubles every 3 hours. How many bacteria will there be after 15 hours?

Doubling means the population multiplies by 2 every 3 hours.
Number of doubling periods in 15 hours = 15 ÷ 3 = 5
P5 = 500 × 25 = 500 × 32 = 16 000

After 15 hours there will be 16 000 bacteria.

4. Finding the Growth Rate

Sometimes we know the population at two different times and need to find the annual growth rate.

r = (Pn ÷ P0)1/n − 1

Example 3 – Calculating Growth Rate

In 2015 a city had 80 000 residents. In 2023 the population was 105 000. Find the average annual growth rate.

P0 = 80 000
Pn = 105 000
n = 2023 − 2015 = 8 years
r = (105 000 ÷ 80 000)1/8 − 1
r = (1.3125)0.125 − 1
r ≈ 1.0347 − 1 = 0.0347 or 3.47%

The average annual growth rate is approximately 3.47%.

5. Doubling Time

The doubling time is the time it takes for a population to become twice its current size.

Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ (growth rate in %)

This is known as the Rule of 70 (a useful approximation).

Example 4 – Doubling Time

A population is growing at 2.5% per year. Approximately how long will it take to double?

Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ 2.5 = 28 years
Exact formula: Using logarithms, the exact doubling time is
n = log(2) ÷ log(1 + r)

6. Continuous Growth Model (Optional)

When growth is considered continuous rather than in discrete periods, we use the exponential growth formula:

Pt = P0 × ert

where e ≈ 2.71828 (Euler�s number) and t is time.

Example 5 – Continuous Growth

A population of 5 000 grows continuously at 3% per year. What will the population be after 10 years?

P10 = 5 000 × e0.03 × 10
P10 = 5 000 × e0.3
P10 = 5 000 × 1.34986 ≈ 6 749

7. Limitations of the Geometric Growth Model

While geometric (exponential) growth is a powerful model, it has important limitations:

  • It assumes unlimited resources (food, space, etc.).
  • In reality, populations eventually face limiting factors and growth slows down.
  • The model does not account for sudden events such as disease, natural disasters, or migration.
  • For long-term predictions, more advanced models (such as the logistic growth model) are often used.
Remember: Geometric growth models are excellent for short-term and medium-term projections, but they should be used carefully for very long periods.

8. Summary of Key Formulas

Future population:   Pn = P0 × (1 + r)n
Growth rate:   r = (Pn ÷ P0)1/n − 1
Doubling time (approx):   70 ÷ (growth rate %)
Continuous growth:   Pt = P0 × ert

9. Practice Questions

  1. A village has 8 500 people and is growing at 3.2% per year. What will the population be after 5 years?
  2. A bacterial culture doubles every 4 hours. Starting with 200 bacteria, how many will there be after 20 hours?
  3. In 2010 a country had 45 million people. In 2020 it had 52 million. Calculate the average annual growth rate.
  4. A population is growing at 1.8% per year. Approximately how many years will it take to double?
  5. A city of 30 000 is decreasing in population by 1.5% each year due to migration. What will the population be after 8 years?
