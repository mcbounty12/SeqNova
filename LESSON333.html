Depreciation

Lesson 03  •  Understanding How Assets Lose Value Over Time

1. What is Depreciation?

Depreciation is the gradual decrease in the value of an asset over time due to wear and tear, age, or obsolescence. Almost every physical asset cars, machines, computers, furniture, and buildings loses value as time passes.

In mathematics, when an asset loses value by a fixed percentage of its current value each year, the successive values form a geometric sequence with a common ratio less than 1.

There are two common methods of calculating depreciation:

  • Straight-Line Method the asset loses the same amount of value every year.
  • Reducing-Balance (Declining-Balance) Method the asset loses a fixed percentage of its current value every year. This method produces a geometric sequence.

In this lesson we focus on the Reducing-Balance Method because it is modeled directly by geometric sequences and series.

2. The Reducing-Balance Depreciation Model

Let:

  • V0 = original (purchase) value of the asset
  • r = annual depreciation rate (as a decimal)
  • n = number of years
  • Vn = value of the asset after n years (also called the book value)

The value after n years is given by:

Vn = V0 × (1 − r)n

Here the common ratio of the geometric sequence is (1 − r), which is always between 0 and 1 when the asset is depreciating.

Note: The amount of depreciation in any year is not constant. It is largest in the first year and becomes smaller in later years.

3. Finding the Value After n Years

Example 1 – Car Depreciation

A car is bought for $25 000. It depreciates at 15% per year. What will the car be worth after 5 years?

Given:
V0 = 25 000
r = 15% = 0.15
n = 5
Solution:
V5 = 25 000 × (1 − 0.15)5
V5 = 25 000 × (0.85)5
V5 = 25 000 × 0.443705
V5 ≈ $11 093

After 5 years the car will be worth approximately $11 093.

Example 2 – Machine Depreciation

A machine costs $48 000 and depreciates at 12% per annum on the reducing-balance method. Find its value after 4 years.

V4 = 48 000 × (1 − 0.12)4
V4 = 48 000 × (0.88)4
V4 = 48 000 × 0.599695
V4 ≈ $28 785

4. Finding the Depreciation Rate

Sometimes we know the original value and the value after a certain number of years, and we need to find the annual depreciation rate.

r = 1 − (Vn ÷ V0)1/n

Example 3 – Finding the Rate

A computer was purchased for $1 800. After 3 years its book value is $900. Calculate the annual depreciation rate.

V0 = 1 800
V3 = 900
n = 3
r = 1 − (900 ÷ 1 800)1/3
r = 1 − (0.5)1/3
r = 1 − 0.7937
r ≈ 0.2063 or 20.63%

The computer depreciates at approximately 20.63% per year.

5. Scrap Value (Residual Value)

The scrap value (or residual value) is the estimated value of an asset at the end of its useful life. It is the lowest value the asset is expected to reach.

Example 4 – Time to Reach Scrap Value

A piece of equipment costs $12 000 and depreciates at 20% per year. Its scrap value is $1 500. After how many full years will its value first fall below $1 500?

We solve: 12 000 × (0.80)n < 1 500
(0.80)n < 1 500 ÷ 12 000
(0.80)n < 0.125
Take logarithms:
n × log(0.80) < log(0.125)
n > log(0.125) ÷ log(0.80)   (sign reverses because log(0.80) is negative)
n > 9.64

The value first falls below $1 500 after 10 years.

6. Comparing Straight-Line and Reducing-Balance Methods

Although both methods reduce the value of an asset, they behave differently:

  • Straight-Line equal amount of depreciation each year. Easy to calculate but less realistic for many assets.
  • Reducing-Balance higher depreciation in the early years and lower depreciation later. This often matches the real loss of value more closely (especially for cars and technology).
Why reducing-balance is geometric:
Each year the value is multiplied by the same factor (1 − r). This repeated multiplication is exactly the definition of a geometric sequence.

7. Real-Life Applications

  • Vehicles cars and trucks lose a large percentage of their value in the first few years.
  • Electronics phones, laptops and computers become outdated quickly.
  • Industrial Machinery factories calculate depreciation for accounting and tax purposes.
  • Furniture & Equipment offices and schools track the declining value of assets.
  • Tax & Accounting governments allow businesses to claim depreciation as an expense.

8. Summary of Key Formulas

Value after n years:   Vn = V0 × (1 − r)n
Depreciation rate:   r = 1 − (Vn ÷ V0)1/n
Depreciation in year k:   Dk = V0 × r × (1 − r)k−1

9. Practice Questions

  1. A motorcycle is bought for $9 500 and depreciates at 18% per year. What will it be worth after 4 years?
  2. A printer costs $2 400. After 5 years its book value is $600. Calculate the annual depreciation rate.
  3. A company buys a machine for $60 000. It depreciates at 10% per annum on the reducing-balance method. Find the book value after 6 years.
  4. An asset purchased for $15 000 has a scrap value of $2 000. It depreciates at 25% per year. After how many full years will its value first drop below the scrap value?
  5. A laptop costs $1 200 and loses 22% of its value each year. How much depreciation occurs in the third year?
