Population Growth

Models & Predictions Using Sequences and Series

Learn how geometric sequences describe year-by-year population change and how we use them to predict future growth.

1 Why Model Population Growth?

Populations of people, animals, and even bacteria rarely stay the same. When the increase happens at a roughly constant percentage each year, the numbers form a clear mathematical pattern. That pattern is a geometric sequence.

By turning population change into a sequence, we can predict how large a group will be in 5, 10, or 20 years and understand the story behind the numbers.

2 The Geometric Growth Model

Suppose a population starts at P0 and grows by a fixed percentage r each year. After one year the population becomes P0 × (1 + r). After two years it becomes P0 × (1 + r)2, and so on.

Pn = P0 × (1 + r)n P0 = starting population  |  r = annual growth rate (decimal)  |  n = years

This is exactly the same idea as the geometric sequence formula:

an = a1 × rn−1 Here the first term a1 = P0 and the common ratio is (1 + r)

3 Seeing the Sequence Year by Year

Each year produces the next term of the sequence. The list of populations is itself a geometric sequence.

Example A Growing Town

A town starts with 10 000 people and grows at 5% per year.

  • Common ratio = 1 + 0.05 = 1.05
  • P0 = 10 000
Year (n) Term Calculation Population
0 P0 10 000 × 1.00 10 000
1 P1 10 000 × 1.05 10 500
2 P2 10 000 × (1.05)2 11 025
3 P3 10 000 × (1.05)3 11 576
5 P5 10 000 × (1.05)5 12 763
10 P10 10 000 × (1.05)10 16 289

The sequence of populations is:   10 000, 10 500, 11 025, 11 576, … Each term is the previous term multiplied by 1.05.

4 Predicting Population Over Time

Once you know P0 and r, you can jump to any future year without calculating every single term. That is the power of the closed-form formula.

Prediction Example

Using the same town (P0 = 10 000, r = 5%):

  1. After 15 years: P15 = 10 000 × (1.05)15 ≈ 20 789
  2. After 20 years: P20 = 10 000 × (1.05)20 ≈ 26 533

The model predicts the town will more than double in about 15 years.

Forward Prediction

Given today’s population and growth rate, estimate the size in any future year using Pn = P0 (1 + r)n.

Working Backwards

Given two population figures and the time between them, you can solve for the average growth rate r.

5 Where Do Series Appear?

A sequence gives the population at each year. A series is the sum of terms. In population studies we sometimes care about totals over many years for example total births, or cumulative growth. The sum of a geometric sequence is a geometric series:

Sn = P0 [(1 + r)n − 1] ÷ r    (when r ≠ 0) Useful when you need the sum of successive population values or related quantities

For pure size prediction we usually only need the single term Pn. The series becomes important when we add quantities that themselves follow geometric growth.

6 Assumptions and Limitations

The simple geometric model is powerful, but it rests on a few assumptions:

Remember: Real populations are affected by migration, changing birth rates, resources, disease, and policy. The geometric model is a useful starting point, not a perfect forecast.
  • Growth rate stays constant every year
  • No sudden events (wars, pandemics, large migration)
  • Resources do not limit growth (unlimited environment)
  • We treat population as a continuous smooth number

More advanced models (logistic growth, age-structured models) add extra terms, but they still build on the same geometric foundation.

7 Key Ideas to Take Away

Constant % Growth

A fixed percentage increase each year creates a geometric sequence

Year = Term

Each year’s population is the next term of the sequence

Prediction Power

The closed formula lets you jump to any future year instantly

Series for Totals

Summing geometric terms gives cumulative quantities over time

Quick Summary

  • Population growth at a constant rate is modelled by a geometric sequence.
  • The key formula is Pn = P0 × (1 + r)n.
  • Each year multiplies the previous population by the common ratio (1 + r).
  • We can predict future size, find the growth rate, or solve for time.
  • Series appear when we need the sum of successive population-related values.
  • The model is a clear starting point; real life may need more complex adjustments.
