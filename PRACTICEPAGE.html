Lesson 1 Geometric Sequences & Series

Practice the basics: nth term, common ratio, and sums

1 Finding the common ratio

A geometric sequence begins: 5, 15, 45, 135, … Find the common ratio r.

Hint: Divide any term by the previous term. r = a2 ÷ a1 = a3 ÷ a2 = …
Example: For 2, 6, 18, 54 … r = 6 ÷ 2 = 3, and 18 ÷ 6 = 3. So r = 3.
Answer: r = 15 ÷ 5 = 3. Check: 45 ÷ 15 = 3, 135 ÷ 45 = 3. Common ratio is 3.

2 Finding the nth term

For the geometric sequence with a1 = 3 and r = 2, find the 6th term a6.

Hint: Use an = a1 × rn−1. Here n = 6, so a6 = 3 × 25.
Example: a1 = 4, r = 3, find a4. a4 = 4 × 33 = 4 × 27 = 108.
Answer: a6 = 3 × 25 = 3 × 32 = 96.

3 Sum of the first n terms

Find S5 for the geometric sequence: 2, 6, 18, 54, 162 … (Use Sn = a1 (rn − 1) ÷ (r − 1).)

Hint: a1 = 2, r = 3, n = 5. S5 = 2 (35 − 1) ÷ (3 − 1).
Example: a1 = 1, r = 2, n = 4. S4 = 1 (24 − 1) ÷ (2 − 1) = 15.
Answer: S5 = 2 (243 − 1) ÷ 2 = 2 × 242 ÷ 2 = 242.

4 Writing the general term

Write the formula for the nth term of the sequence: 7, 14, 28, 56, …

Hint: Find a1 and r, then write an = a1 × rn−1.
Example: 3, 6, 12, 24 … → a1 = 3, r = 2 → an = 3 × 2n−1.
Answer: a1 = 7, r = 2. an = 7 × 2n−1.

5 Identifying a geometric sequence

Which of these is a geometric sequence? (A) 2, 4, 6, 8    (B) 3, 9, 27, 81    (C) 5, 10, 20, 35

Hint: In a geometric sequence the ratio between consecutive terms is constant.
Example: 4, 8, 16, 32 has r = 2 throughout → geometric. 4, 7, 10, 13 has a common difference, not ratio → arithmetic.
Answer: (B) 3, 9, 27, 81 � common ratio r = 3. (A) is arithmetic (d = 2). (C) ratios are not constant (10/5 = 2, but 35/20 = 1.75).

Lesson 2 Population Growth

Practice growth models: Pn = P0 × (1 + r)n

1 Future population

A town has 8 000 people and grows at 5% per year. What is the population after 3 years? (Round to the nearest whole number.)

Hint: Pn = P0 × (1 + r)n. P0 = 8000, r = 0.05, n = 3.
Example: 5 000 people, 4% growth, 2 years. P2 = 5000 × (1.04)2 = 5000 × 1.0816 = 5 408.
Answer: P3 = 8000 × (1.05)3 = 8000 × 1.157625 = 9 261.

2 Finding the growth rate

A population grew from 12 000 to 15 972 in 4 years. Find the annual growth rate as a percentage (1 decimal place).

Hint: r = (Pn ÷ P0)1/n − 1, then × 100 for %.
Example: From 1000 to 1464 in 3 years. r = (1.464)1/3 − 1 ≈ 0.135 → 13.5%.
Answer: r = (15972 ÷ 12000)1/4 − 1 = (1.331)0.25 − 1 ≈ 0.074 → 7.4%.

3 Doubling time (approximate)

A bacteria culture doubles every year. If it starts with 200 bacteria, how many are there after 5 years?

Hint: Doubling means r = 1 (100% growth), so (1 + r) = 2. Pn = 200 × 2n.
Example: Start 50, double each year, after 3 years: 50 × 23 = 400.
Answer: P5 = 200 × 25 = 200 × 32 = 6 400.

4 One-year growth

A city has 50 000 people. If the growth rate is 2.5% per year, what is the population after 1 year?

Hint: After 1 year: P1 = P0 × (1 + r) = 50000 × 1.025.
Example: 10 000 at 3%: 10000 × 1.03 = 10 300.
Answer: 50 000 × 1.025 = 51 250.

5 Interpreting the model

In the formula Pn = 2000 × (1.06)n, what do 2000 and 1.06 represent?

Hint: Compare with Pn = P0 × (1 + r)n.
Example: Pn = 500 × (1.03)n means initial population 500 and 3% growth per year.
Answer: 2000 is the initial population P0. 1.06 is (1 + r), so the growth rate is 6% per year.

Lesson 3 Depreciation

Practice reducing-balance: Vn = V0 × (1 − r)n

1 Book value after n years

A car costs $25 000 and depreciates at 12% per year. Find its book value after 2 years (nearest dollar).

Hint: Vn = V0 × (1 − r)n. V0 = 25000, r = 0.12, n = 2 → (0.88)2.
Example: $10 000 at 10% for 2 years: 10000 × (0.9)2 = 10000 × 0.81 = $8 100.
Answer: V2 = 25000 × (0.88)2 = 25000 × 0.7744 = $19 360.

2 Value after one year

A machine costs $8 000 and depreciates at 15% per year. What is its value at the end of the first year?

Hint: After 1 year: V1 = V0 × (1 − r) = 8000 × 0.85.
Example: $5 000 at 20%: 5000 × 0.80 = $4 000.
Answer: 8000 × 0.85 = $6 800.

3 Finding the depreciation rate

An asset bought for $16 000 is worth $8 192 after 3 years. Find the annual depreciation rate (nearest whole percent).

Hint: r = 1 − (Vn ÷ V0)1/n. Then multiply by 100.
Example: $9 000 → $5 832 in 2 years. r = 1 − (5832/9000)1/2 = 1 − 0.805 ≈ 0.195 → 19.5%.
Answer: r = 1 − (8192/16000)1/3 = 1 − (0.512)1/3 = 1 − 0.8 = 0.2 → 20%.

4 Three-year depreciation table

A laptop costs $1 200 and depreciates at 25% per year. Find its book value at the end of year 1, year 2 and year 3.

Hint: Multiply by 0.75 each year (since 1 − 0.25 = 0.75).
Example: $400 at 10%: Year 1: 360; Year 2: 324; Year 3: 291.60.
Answer: Year 1: 1200 × 0.75 = $900 Year 2: 900 × 0.75 = $675 Year 3: 675 × 0.75 = $506.25

5 Comparing growth and decay

Why is the common ratio for depreciation written as (1 − r) instead of (1 + r)?

Hint: Think about whether the value is increasing or decreasing each year.
Example: Growth 5% → multiply by 1.05. Decay 5% → multiply by 0.95 (keep 95% of the value).
Answer: Depreciation means the value falls, so each year we keep only a fraction of the previous value. (1 − r) is less than 1 (e.g. 0.85 for 15% depreciation). (1 + r) is greater than 1 and is used for growth, not decay.
